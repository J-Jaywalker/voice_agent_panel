# Moderator Cue Card — Ricky

**"Agentic Adoption: The Revenge of the Humans"** · Boost Camp Oslo, 21 Oct 2026

One page. What to say, roughly, and in what order. The full material — what each
agent believes and the anecdotes behind it — is in
[`beat-sheet.md`](beat-sheet.md); you shouldn't need it on stage.

---

## Three rules

1. **Ask a question. Statements invite nobody.** If you make an observation and
   pause, nothing happens — the floor is closed by default.
2. **Use a name.** "Wayne, what do you think?" not "thoughts?"
3. **To stop an agent, just talk.** You outrank all three. No need to be polite
   about it.

Avoid: "…is that okay?", "…right?", "…does that work?" — question-shaped, but
they invite nobody.

Each beat runs itself for 60–90 seconds once opened. Let it. You only need to
speak again to move on, to redirect, or to cut someone off.

## Beat by beat — what comes back

**0 · Introductions** — fixed, pre-recorded wording, ~20s each in order Dexter,
Melia, Wayne. Nothing can interrupt this round. Just let it run.

**1 · The curve.** Wayne says we're further along than the room admits and
offers that his board stopped asking to see his working. Dexter calls that
fatigue, not adoption. Melia says the slide says complete and the ops floor is
still doing it by hand. *Nobody concedes — that's fine, don't resolve it.*
→ **Out:** *"Dexter, what's actually changed?"*

**2 · What changed.** Dexter: it got cheap, not clever — the ideas sat in papers
for years. He agrees with Wayne on mechanism and needles him on timing. Wayne:
price is what moves a business. Melia: it got easy to deploy without asking
permission, which accelerates the incidents too.
→ **Out:** *"Melia, what's going to hold it back?"*

**3 · What holds it back.** The sharpest beat. Melia: not capability — she wrote
a caveat that made the appendix and the sign-off quoted the summary. Wayne
attacks the delay, she comes back with *"you have a board that stopped asking
you questions."* Dexter lands it technically: nobody can tell you what the agent
did last Thursday. Melia plants the ops team who ran the old process in parallel
for eight months.
*Let her comeback land. Don't rescue Wayne.*
→ **Out:** *"Dexter, what's still genuinely hard in speech recognition?"*

**4 · STT. ⚠️ Your beat.** They describe the problems: knowing when a person has
actually finished when the audio is identical either way, and names the system
has never heard getting silently replaced. Melia gets the laugh — *"half the
systems I talk to call me Amelia."* Wayne says it's all got dates on it.

**They cannot answer any of it.** The agents know nothing about Speechmatics by
design, and if asked they'll hand it back to the humans in the room. Bank the
problems here and answer them in your close.

**If one starts making a product claim: cut in.** *"Dexter, hold on."*
→ **Out:** *"Dexter, are people actually trying to break these things?"*

**5 · Jailbreaking.** Dexter: constantly, and the ones that work aren't clever,
they're *polite*. Melia reframes it — a lot of it is people routing round a
system that won't let them do their job. Wayne unexpectedly agrees: if your own
staff are jailbreaking it, the policy is the bug. Dexter concedes warmly.
*This is where the title pays off. Don't rush it.*
→ **Out:** *"So Wayne, what's actually been solved?"*

**6 · What's solved.** Wayne: the boring things, which is the point. Dexter
concedes real ground — he'd have flagged real-time speech as a deployment risk
and no longer does. Melia closes: a team who finally switched the parallel
process off after eight months, *"the only adoption number I trust, and it isn't
on anybody's dashboard."*

**Then you close.** Melia's line is your hand-off.

## If it goes wrong

| | Say |
|---|---|
| Nothing happened | You made a statement. Re-cue with a name and a question. |
| Wrong agent answered | *"Can Melia take that one?"* |
| Won't stop talking | Don't reach for this before ~60s in — there's no enforced cap, let it run. Past that, just talk. Or *"Wayne, wrap up."* |
| Heading for a product claim | *"Dexter, hold on."* — then take it yourself. |
| Beat died | Skip to the next cue. Only Beats 3 and 6 are linked. |
| An agent is down | Two is still a panel. Dexter covers 4 and 5, Wayne 1, 2 and 6, Melia 3. |
| Everything is down | Your solo segment. |

---

## Running long or short

**Long — cut in this order:** Beat 2 (Dexter and Wayne broadly agree), then
Beat 1 (positions re-establish themselves in Beat 3 anyway).

**Short — extend with:** *"Melia, do you agree with Dexter?"* or
*"Wayne, finish your point."* Beats 3 and 5 have the most left in them.

**Never cut:** Beat 4 (it sets up your close) or Beat 6 (it is the close).
