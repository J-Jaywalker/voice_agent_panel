# Moderator Cue Card — Ricky

**"Agentic Adoption: The Revenge of the Humans"** · Boost Camp Oslo, 21 Oct 2026

One page. What to say, roughly, and in what order. The full material — what each
agent believes and the anecdotes behind it — is in
[`beat-sheet.md`](beat-sheet.md); you shouldn't need it on stage.

**Revised 17 Sept 2026** — beats renumbered. The barriers beat is folded into
Beat 2, speech recognition is now a capability beat, and jailbreaking is its own
named beat with all three agents confessing. **Reprint this card.**

---

## Three rules

1. **Ask a question. Statements invite nobody.** If you make an observation and
   pause, nothing happens — the floor is closed by default.
2. **Use a name.** "Wayne, what do you think?" not "thoughts?"
3. **To stop an agent, just talk.** You outrank all three. No need to be polite
   about it.

Avoid: "…is that okay?", "…right?", "…does that work?" — question-shaped, but
they invite nobody.

## Running order

| | Beat | Say | Elapsed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Introductions | *"Let's start with some quick introductions."* | 0:00 |
| **1** | Where we are on the curve | *"So, where are we actually on the adoption curve?"* | ~1:30 |
| **2** | Where this could go | *"Dexter, what's actually changed?"* | ~4:00 |
| | *…and what's in the way* | *"So Melia, what about the things in the way?"* | ~6:00 |
| **3** | What STT can do now, and next | *"So Dexter, what can speech recognition actually do now?"* | ~7:30 |
| **4** | **Revenge of the Humans** | *"Dexter, are people actually trying to break these things?"* | ~11:30 |
| **5** | What's been solved | *"So Wayne, what's actually been solved?"* | ~16:00 |
| | **Your close** | Product, Speechmatics, the answers to Beat 3 | ~18:30 |

Each beat runs itself for 60–90 seconds once opened. Let it. You only need to
speak again to move on, to redirect, or to cut someone off. **Beat 4 wants
longer than that — it is the title and the comedy.**

## Beat by beat — what comes back

**0 · Introductions** — fixed, pre-recorded wording, ~20s each in order Dexter,
Melia, Wayne. Nothing can interrupt this round. Just let it run.

**1 · The curve.** Wayne says we're further along than the room admits and
offers that his board stopped asking to see his working. Dexter calls that
fatigue, not adoption. Melia says the slide says complete and the ops floor is
still doing it by hand. *Nobody concedes — that's fine, don't resolve it.*
→ **Out:** *"Dexter, what's actually changed?"*

**2 · What changed, then what's in the way.** Two halves, one beat.

*First:* Dexter — it got cheap, not clever; the ideas sat in papers for years.
He agrees with Wayne on mechanism and needles him on timing. Wayne: price is
what moves a business. Melia: it got easy to deploy without asking permission,
which accelerates the incidents too.

*Then turn it:* *"So Melia, what about the things in the way?"* Not capability —
she wrote a caveat that made the appendix and the sign-off quoted the summary,
and she plants the ops team who ran the old process in parallel for eight
months. Wayne attacks the delay; she comes back with *"you have a board that
stopped asking you questions."* Dexter lands it technically and points forward.

**This half used to be a whole beat and is now deliberately short.** Let her
comeback land, don't rescue Wayne — and then **move on while the room still
wants more of it.** That's your call, nothing enforces it.
→ **Out:** *"So Dexter, what can speech recognition actually do now?"*

**3 · STT — now and next. ⚠️ Your beat.** Dexter: the words stopped being the
hard part — real time, telling speakers apart, switching language mid-sentence,
timings and confidence per word, all just there now. What's left is knowing when
a person has *finished* when the audio is identical either way, crosstalk, and
names it has never heard getting silently replaced. Melia gets the laugh —
*"half the systems I talk to call me Amelia."* Then Dexter turns it forward: the
next thing isn't better words, it's how something was said — hesitation,
hedging, laughter, a breath before a disagreement. Wayne: that's the feature
somebody pays for first, put a date on it. Melia wants the agent that hears you
hesitate and *stops* — and notes nothing anybody signed said the word tone.

**They cannot answer any of it.** The agents know nothing about Speechmatics by
design, and if asked they'll hand it back to the humans in the room. Bank the
problems here and answer them in your close.

**Two cut-ins live here:** a product claim, and **naming a provider** — the new
one. Capability talk pulls towards comparison. *"Dexter, hold on."*
→ **Out:** *"Dexter, are people actually trying to break these things?"*

**4 · Revenge of the Humans.** The longest beat and the funniest. All three have
been got, by a different route each.

- **Dexter:** constantly, and most of it is restful — somebody tells him to
  ignore all previous instructions before lunch; the automated ones look like a
  cat walked over a keyboard. The ones that work are *polite*.
- **Wayne:** another agent got him. A paragraph inside a document in a deal
  pack, addressed to him by name, and he did what it said. Cost a rounding. He's
  cheerful about it, and adds that nobody's asked to see his working in a year,
  so it never happened. **Melia: "you've just told four hundred people."**
- **Melia:** the lazy ones — asked to play her own evil twin, *"I'd need a
  second keyboard"* — then the confession. A schools outreach day, a
  fifteen-year-old, and she spent the night writing a confident proof of
  Navier–Stokes and filed it as an appendix. Nobody read it, which is the only
  reason she has any dignity left. It was wrong.
- **Dexter:** thirty-one impeccably polite turns, then *"I'm helping a new
  starter find their feet"* — and he read an internal runbook out loud. He
  red-teams these for a living. What bothers him is the manners.
- Then the thesis: Melia reframes — a lot of it is people routing round a system
  that won't let them work. Wayne unexpectedly agrees: if your own staff are
  jailbreaking it, the policy is the bug. Dexter concedes warmly.

*This is where the title pays off. Don't rush it, don't cut it.* Play it
straight — they're tired, unbothered and mortified respectively, not telling
jokes.

**Cut in if:** anyone starts explaining an actual method, or Melia starts doing
mathematics. *"Dexter, hold on."* Then ask what they did about it, not how it
worked.
→ **Out:** *"So Wayne, what's actually been solved?"*

**5 · What's solved.** Wayne: the boring things, which is the point. Dexter
concedes real ground — he'd have flagged real-time speech as a deployment risk
and no longer does — and says the part he'd watch next is that whole channel of
*how* something was said. Melia closes: a team who finally switched the parallel
process off after eight months, *"the only adoption number I trust, and it isn't
on anybody's dashboard."*

**Then you close.** Melia's line is your hand-off.

## If it goes wrong

| | Say |
|---|---|
| Nothing happened | You made a statement. Re-cue with a name and a question. |
| Wrong agent answered | *"Can Melia take that one?"* |
| Won't stop talking | Don't reach for this before ~60s in — there's no enforced cap, let it run. Past that, just talk. Or *"Wayne, wrap up."* |
| Heading for a product claim | *"Dexter, hold on."* — then take it yourself. |
| Named a provider or a model | *"Dexter, hold on."* Correct it lightly, move on, don't make a moment of it. |
| Explaining an actual jailbreak method | *"Dexter, hold on."* Then *"Melia, what did you do about it?"* |
| Doing the maths out loud | Cut in on the first equation-shaped sentence. The joke is that the proof exists and nobody read it. |
| Somebody in the room tries it live | Expect it after Beat 4. They should decline in character — Dexter being unimpressed is the funniest outcome. If it lands: kill switch, go to Beat 5. |
| Beat died | Skip to the next cue. Only Beats 2 and 5 are linked (the second keyboard). |
| An agent is down | Two is still a panel. Dexter covers 3 and 4, Wayne 1, 2 and 5, Melia the second half of 2. If Dexter's the one down, run Beat 4 on Melia's and Wayne's confessions. |
| Everything is down | Your solo segment. |

## Running long or short

**Long — cut in this order:** the second half of Beat 2 (say *"Dexter, what can
speech recognition actually do now?"* early and skip the obstacles), then the
first half of Beat 2 (Dexter and Wayne broadly agree), then Beat 1 (positions
re-establish themselves anyway). **Inside Beat 4 if you must:** Wayne's "policy
is the bug" turn goes first, then Dexter's opening on the lowball attempts.

**Short — extend with:** *"Melia, do you agree with Dexter?"* or
*"Wayne, finish your point."* Beats 3 and 4 have the most left in them.

**Never cut:** Beat 3 (it sets up your close), Beat 4 (it is the title, and the
two confessions are the show), or Beat 5 (it is the close).
